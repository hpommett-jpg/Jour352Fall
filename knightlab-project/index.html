NASA Images Show Decades of Change

Some of the clearest records of change come from NASA cameras. Satellites have tracked the growth of American cities for decades, and more recently astronauts documented three decades of human spaceflight aboard the space shuttle. The features below use NASA imagery to show the changes in different places and across different eras.

Las Vegas spreads across the desert

Las Vegas has pushed steadily into the desert around it and become one of the fastest-growing metropolitan areas in the United States. The metro population reached about 2.2 million in 2017, more than eight times its 1972 population. Drag the slider to compare a Landsat 1 image taken Sept. 13, 1972, with a Landsat 8 image taken April 11, 2018. In these false-color images, urban development appears green and purple.

The space shuttle's 30-year run

NASA's space shuttle program flew 135 missions from 1981 to 2011. Columbia opened the era April 12, 1981, when astronauts John Young and Robert Crippen launched on STS-1. The program also endured two disasters. Challenger broke apart Jan. 28, 1986, and Columbia broke apart Feb. 1, 2003, and each accident killed all seven crew members aboard. The era ended July 21, 2011, when Atlantis landed to complete STS-135. In April 2012, Discovery went on display at the Smithsonian's Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center in Chantilly, Virginia.
